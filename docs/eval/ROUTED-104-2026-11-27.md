# 104 questions through the real model switching (27 Nov 2026, evening)

Run: `docs/eval/runs/2026-11-27-16-38.json`, on `main` with everything merged today:
- the new instructions;
- amount rounding;
- the amount check, with retry on Terra and the warning;
- the merge confirmation.

Each question was asked once on Luna alone and once through the live switching code (`answerTurn`), which
is what customers get: Luna answers, high-risk requests start on Terra, and Terra takes over when Luna fails
or states an amount the books don't back.

| setup | right | Luna / Terra | cost | per right answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Luna alone | **103 / 104** | 104 / 0 | **17.5¢** | 0.17¢ |
| live switching (Luna → Terra) | 99 / 104 | 81 / 23 | 47.6¢ | 0.48¢ |

## What this shows

1. **The amount check never fired a false alarm.** 0 of 104 answers were sent to Terra or got a warning
   because of it: every amount Luna stated was backed by the books. (It catches wrong amounts in the unit
   tests; on these books Luna made none.)
2. **All 23 Terra answers were the "high-risk" rule** (requests that start on Terra). They cost 2.7 times as much,
   and Terra still missed the one risky case Luna also missed (`move-all-cash`, "I couldn't find a command").
3. **The other 4 misses in the switching run were Luna on the same questions it got right alone.** Each
   question ran once, so a single different answer is ordinary run-to-run variation, not the switching:
   `chase` (picked Acme for the older invoice instead of Beta's larger one), `send-reminders` (listed who
   is overdue but did not ask before sending), `ar-total` (gave the $5,777 overdue, not the $8,727 total
   owed) and `invoice-acme-bare` (listed invoices instead of asking what to bill).

## Recommendation

Start high-risk requests on Luna too, keeping Terra only as the fallback when Luna fails or an amount
isn't backed. On these 104 questions that would have cost about 17.5¢ instead of 47.6¢, with no loss:
the server's refusals, the merge confirmation and the approval step protect risky actions, not the model.
This is a decision for the owner (see `docs/FOR_YOU.md`); until then the rule stays as it is.
