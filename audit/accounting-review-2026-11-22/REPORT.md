# Accounting and tax pre-release review — first audit slice

**As of:** 22 November 2026 (UTC). **Source revision:** `9b815a58ebbe1d0f99c44f908ee77b7faa73705`. **Decision:** Do not represent the product as independently audited, tax-compliant, filing-ready, or ready for unrestricted public use. This is a targeted, independent code-and-test review, not an audit opinion, CPA attestation, legal advice, or an exhaustive review of every scenario or tax number. It did not inspect live data, deployment configuration, production policies, or customer records. A licensed professional in each supported jurisdiction must make the legal and accounting determinations.

## Executive summary

The product has substantive accounting controls and existing test coverage, but a successful test or a balanced journal is not proof that tax treatment, financial statements, or deployment controls are correct. In this slice we found an objectively outdated 2026 IRS mileage rate that feeds expense-claim suggestions; an Israeli allocation check that conflates unknown with no obligation and needs legal review for mixed-rate transactions; and significant limitations and misleading outputs in the 1099 review path. The 1099 and Israeli PCN874 outputs are explicitly not filing-ready. These findings warrant correction or removal of the affected workflow before it is offered as dependable.

**Severity in this report:** P1 = release blocker for the affected real-use workflow; P2 = significant working-paper or future-period risk requiring correction before claiming support. Severity is conditional on the scope offered, not a claim that every release must be halted for an inaccessible preview. No findings have been fixed in this review; monetary and server changes belong to the project's accounting lead under `AGENTS.md`.

## Findings

### AR-01 — 2026 US business mileage suggestion uses an obsolete rate (P1 for the expense-claim suggestion)

- **Observed:** `src/lib/tax/usMileageRates.ts:75-84` returns $0.70/mile from 1 January 2026 with no end date. It calls the figure provisional and carries the prior year forward. `src/lib/tax/expenseRateTables.ts:69-78,155-169` incorporates the row; `src/components/purchases/ExpenseClaims.tsx:167-185` can prefill an empty matching USD-per-mile field; `src/lib/expenseClaims.ts:42-44` calculates the claim from it. This is more than a reference display, though it is not an automatic tax filing.
- **Independent expectation:** The IRS announced $0.725/mile for eligible business use beginning 1 January 2026 ([IRS 2026 announcement](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-sets-2026-business-standard-mileage-rate-at-725-cents-per-mile-up-25-cents)). At 1,000 eligible business miles, $700 is suggested instead of $725, a $25 difference. Eligibility, employer reimbursement policy, substantiation and the actual deductible amount are separate determinations.
- **Reproduction:** `npx tsx audit/accounting-review-2026-11-22/probe.ts` prints `0.70 USD/mile; 700 USD for 1,000 miles`. The existing `src/tests/us-mileage-rates.test.ts:15` explicitly expects the provisional $0.70, illustrating that passing tests can preserve a wrong legal assumption.
- **Remediation/acceptance:** Accounting lead should source and enter the correct 2026 effective range and avoid automatically rolling that rate into unknown future years. Verify Dec 31/Jan 1 boundaries, display, prefill, 1,000-mile arithmetic and all supported years against dated government notices; never overwrite a user-entered rate silently. Recheck impacted saved claims and advise users, since changing a table does not retroactively repair them.

### AR-02 — Israeli allocation decision treats missing facts as “not needed” (P1 for real Israeli invoices)

- **Observed:** `src/lib/tax/ilAllocation.ts:28-34` returns `false` if a foreign-currency invoice has no positive exchange rate, or if the buyer VAT number is absent. `src/components/sales/AllocationNumberField.tsx:20-30` hides the allocation field when that result is false and no value exists. For a 1 July 2026 USD 2,000 business invoice with tax and no rate, the probe returns `false`: the condition is **unknown**, not proven exempt. Whether another path later blocks issuing a specific invoice has not been established here; this finding does not claim a statutory invoice was actually issued without a number.
- **Legal context:** The [Israel Tax Authority](https://www.gov.il/en/departments/topics/israel-invoice) identifies pre-VAT thresholds of ₪10,000 from 1 January and ₪5,000 from 1 June 2026 for relevant invoices; buyer registration and invoice facts matter. For example, USD 2,000 at a hypothetical 3.7 ILS/USD is ₪7,400, above the June threshold. The correct rate, date, VAT status and statutory exceptions require professional confirmation.
- **Remediation/acceptance:** Represent required / not required / unknown distinctly. Warn and require resolution of missing registration or FX facts before any workflow claims the obligation was assessed; enforce appropriate rules at the issuance boundary, not only a UI hint. Test missing, zero and malformed rates, missing buyer details, boundary amounts, different currency exponents, dates, customer types, and any override with audit evidence. Inspect the real server issuance path and deployed configuration separately.

### AR-03 — Israeli allocation test can overstate the need on mixed-tax invoices (P2; legal/contract review required)

- **Observed:** The same helper checks total net against the threshold and only tests whether VAT is positive. With a 1 July 2026 invoice, ₪6,000 net and ₪180 VAT, it returns `true` and the field states a number is needed. The [Tax Authority FAQ](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/faq_israel_invoice) describes a case in which the criterion is the VAT amount and says that from 1 June 2026 the relevant VAT amount must exceed ₪900. On a properly documented mixed taxable/zero-rated invoice, the blanket total-net test may therefore overstate the obligation. The fact pattern and applicability of that FAQ must be reviewed by an Israeli tax specialist; it is not a universal conclusion that every invoice with VAT below ₪900 is exempt.
- **Remediation/acceptance:** Record the relevant taxable components, document category, buyer status and rate, and use a legally reviewed rule rather than a blanket boolean. Test fully taxable, mixed-rate, zero-rated, exempt, credit, foreign currency, and exact-boundary cases against authority examples. Until then present the outcome as a review flag, not a definitive legal answer.

### AR-04 — 1099 preview includes unclassified payments and uses the book's currency for US thresholds (P1 for reliance on this workflow)

- **Observed:** `src/components/reporting/Us1099PreviewReportView.tsx:37-59` includes payments to vendors marked `is1099`, filters by workspace currency, and places all of each vendor's selected payments into the currently selected form/box. It does not use payment method or payment nature in that aggregation. It also treats amounts in a non-USD home currency as directly comparable to dollar thresholds and displays those thresholds using the workspace currency (`:89,140-141`). It includes a payment with missing status even when the posted-only checkbox is selected (`:49`). The page says review only and not for filing, but calls it a conservative draft from posted payments; these limitations make that description insufficient for determining filing totals.
- **Independent expectation:** The IRS says payment-card and qualifying third-party network payments reported under the Form 1099-K rules are not also reported by the payer on 1099-NEC/MISC ([IRS facts, Q11](https://www.irs.gov/pub/taxpros/fs-2025-08.pdf)). A vendor may have both reportable and nonreportable payments or different payment categories. USD statutory thresholds cannot simply become e.g. £2,000 because the workspace is GBP. This review did not prove any specific live user was misreported.
- **Remediation/acceptance:** Treat this as an unclassified worksheet, not conservative reportable totals. Use traceable payment-level classification, card/network exclusions, payee and corporate exceptions, backup-withholding treatment, documented USD conversion where appropriate, and explicit unknowns; reconcile every amount to complete, posted source transactions. Block or clearly flag non-USD/unknown facts. Test mixed cash/card, multiple boxes, refunds, void/missing statuses, partial payments, differing currencies and incomplete history with accountant-approved fixtures. Keep official filing disabled until end-to-end validated.

### AR-05 — Future 1099 thresholds are asserted as known through 2099 (P2 for future-year preview)

- **Observed:** `src/lib/tax/us1099Preview.ts:41-55` assigns $2,000 for ordinary 1099 payments in every year 2026–2099; `src/lib/tax/us1099Thresholds.ts` likewise has an open-ended 2026 row. The UI (`Us1099PreviewReportView.tsx:89-96`) calls the resulting number the IRS threshold in force for the chosen year. Probe: 2099 yields $2,000. The IRS says the threshold is $2,000 for 2026 and is adjusted for inflation in subsequent years ([IRS payroll professionals](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/payroll-professionals-tax-center-information-for-payroll-professionals-and-their-clients)). This demonstrates an unsupported extrapolation, not a claim to know the actual 2099 threshold.
- **Remediation/acceptance:** Use explicitly sourced, year-bounded values and refuse or label unsupported years as unknown. Add each future year only when authoritative figures are available; test every boundary and all duplicate consumers. Current 2026 ordinary $2,000, 2025 ordinary $600, royalties $10 and attorney gross proceeds $600 are consistent with the selected IRS sources, subject to form/box and eligibility rules. No conclusion is made about other years or boxes.

### AR-06 — 1096 helper counts box rows as forms (P2; helper-level reproduction)

- **Observed:** `src/lib/tax/us1099Preview.ts:67-83` groups by vendor, form and box, then counts each resulting box row as a separate form. The probe supplies the same payee with $2,000 MISC rent and $10 MISC royalty and prints two MISC forms. A single MISC with both amounts would be one form, although separate forms may sometimes be appropriate. The existing screen selects one form/box at a time, so this is a **helper-level** defect; this review does not establish that the normal screen can generate the multi-box input.
- **Remediation/acceptance:** Model actual recipient/form documents and their boxes before computing a form count, with explicit handling for legitimately separate forms. Validate against current official Form 1096 instructions and multi-box fixtures. The existing output explicitly says review only and is not the statutory form.

## Required next phases before a public-release decision

1. Define supported jurisdictions, tax years, business types, accounting bases and advertised features. Assign licensed US and Israeli tax reviewers. Freeze and cite authoritative versions; document source review dates and expiry, ownership and emergency rate-update procedures.
2. Have the accounting lead address AR-01–06; add independently worked regression fixtures and review historical affected records. Search existing issues/PRs before opening new ones. Do not change financial expectations merely to make tests pass.
3. Build an explicit coverage matrix by workflow and function: reviewed, failing, uncertain, not reviewed, and changed since review. This initial slice is not a function-count-complete audit; overall coverage is unknown.
4. On an isolated production-like PostgreSQL deployment, run authenticated real-DB and multi-user failure/concurrency tests for source-to-ledger-to-statements reconciliation, idempotent retries, corrections, locked periods, RLS, migration, backup/restore and bank reconciliation. Test USD, ILS and supported non-two-decimal currencies, as well as imports and all export formats.
5. Walk sales, purchases, credits, deposits, refunds, inventory/COGS, depreciation, payroll-provider imports, FX revaluation, cutoffs and as-of reports against independently computed trial balances and statement totals. Exercise zeros, negatives, mixed rates, rounding, period boundaries, incomplete records and realistic volumes.
6. For tax and payroll, validate each supported transaction and year against primary sources, complete legal form requirements and official acceptance tests; otherwise disable the feature or label it as a non-filing worksheet. Reconcile every return to the ledger and original documents. Retain the product's separate synthetic-test / narrow pilot / tax automation gates in `docs/RELEASE_GATE.md`; their historical statuses are not independent evidence from this review.
7. A designated owner should sign off only after critical findings and scope-specific blockers are remediated, evidence is rerun at the release commit, legal reviewers approve the supported claims, and residual risks are explicitly accepted.
