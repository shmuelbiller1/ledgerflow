# Payroll and Canadian historical tax — partial independent review

**Revision:** `9b815a58ebbe1d0f99c44f908ee77b7fbaa73705`. Sources checked 22 November 2026. These are synthetic, bounded reproductions, not a professional tax opinion, real filing or full payroll audit. Every feature below remains subject to end-to-end and jurisdictional review. Suggested priorities refer to the affected workflow, not every feature in the application.

## PY-01 — Release blocker for actual payroll: forecast tax estimates feed a posting workflow

The payroll engine explicitly disclaims certified tax computation (`src/lib/payroll-engine.ts:6`), and the Payroll screen displays that disclaimer (`PayrollView.tsx:298–303`). Nonetheless `payRunPosting.payRunTotals` takes `computePeriodPayroll` results; `payRunService.postPayRun` calculates those totals and `postRun` posts a journal, stores a processed run, and records per-employee taxes (`src/lib/payRunPosting.ts:50–72`, `src/server/services/payRunService.ts:86–117,139–155`). The screen offers **Post the pay run to the ledger** (`PayrollView.tsx:748–754`). The capability list calls live country payroll calculation internal/demo and says payroll processing is stage C; the real posting path requires an explicit product/operational gate. The documented estimates are not thereby rendered statutory payroll.

Independently worked examples below demonstrate that a balanced journal is not proof of correct tax, pay or liability:

1. **Social Security cap and timing:** $240,000 annual salary, monthly $20,000, no deductions, first January pay and no previous wages. Employee and employer OASDI should each be 6.2% × $20,000 = **$1,240**, as the $184,500 annual cap is not yet reached. The engine smooths the *assumed entire year's* cap across all 12 months and uses a stale $183,000 cap: output **$945.50** each per month. If this identical run is processed 12 times, it totals **$11,346** per side rather than **$11,439** (6.2% × $184,500), $93 low, besides the incorrect early-period withholdings. The `payRunTotals` output and balanced journal are included in `payroll-and-ca-probe.ts`. An independent test posted 11 consecutive synthetic runs through `postPayRun` into an isolated local Postgres database, read back both the processed runs and balanced journal entries, and observed $945.50 Social Security and $290 Medicare in both January and November. It called the service directly; it did not exercise authenticated HTTP or hosted Supabase. SSA confirms the 2026 limit and 6.2% ([SSA taxable maximum](https://www.ssa.gov/OACT/cola/cbb.html)).
2. **401(k) FICA base:** For a conventional employee elective deferral, $5,000 monthly wages with $500 traditional 401(k), below caps and no other adjustments, Social Security should be 6.2% × $5,000 = **$310**, Medicare 1.45% × $5,000 = **$72.50**. The engine outputs **$279** and **$65.25**, also $279 employer Social Security, because it subtracts a generic pre-tax deduction from all tax bases (`payroll-engine.ts:314–335`). The synthetic record uses the same legacy pre-tax deduction represented as a 401(k) in pay-run tests; named `pre_tax` 401(k)s follow the same calculation. Health and other benefit categories have different tax treatment and must not simply be treated identically to 401(k) without policy facts. [IRS Topic 424](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc424) says elective 401(k) deferrals remain Social Security, Medicare and FUTA wages.
3. **Additional Medicare:** A married employee earning $20,000 monthly from this employer, no other wages, reaches $200,000 after month 10; the employer must withhold 0.9% on the $20,000 in month 11, or **$180**, and again in month 12. The computation returns **$0** in every period because annual pay $240,000 is below the married $250,000 *individual* threshold (`payroll-engine.ts:337–346`); it takes no YTD amounts. The [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc560) requires employer withholding above $200,000 regardless of filing status. A separate helper in `usFederalPayroll.ts` has the correct employer threshold and YTD interface, but the posting path does not call it.
4. **Locality:** The engine applies New York City and Kings County percentage estimates to any employee whose only state field is `NY`, with no city residency/work-location fact (`payroll-engine.ts:215–223,359–369`; `Employee` model has state but no tax locality). A synthetic $5,000/month NY record yields **$193.80 city plus $50 county** even if the employee lives and works in Buffalo and is not a NYC resident. New York's [employer guide](https://www.tax.ny.gov/forms/publications/wt/nys50.htm) identifies NYC withholding on NYC resident employees; applicability requires facts, not the state alone. Other local/state rates, W-4 inputs, deductions and NY exceptions need independent review.

The same engine also auto-generates tax-year figures for unsupported years by assuming 3% inflation (`payroll-engine.ts:171–204`), assumes an effective FUTA rate irrespective of credit-reduction status, and uses salary annualization rather than actual YTD for capped taxes. Its 2026 federal tax bracket/deduction values also differ from published values; full reconciliation is outstanding. Do not use this path for actual employee pay/tax until separately controlled and verified. If intended purely for forecasts, prevent it from generating operational pay runs, direct deposit or statutory reports, or require independently calculated verified inputs. For a real payroll implementation, use dated, source-verified jurisdiction tables; tax-specific wage bases and deduction treatments; actual YTD history including prior payroll and reversals; W-4 elections; locality; concurrent/idempotent posting; and qualified payroll review. Preserve the forecast disclaimer rather than presenting it as compliance.

## PY-02 — P1 if this separate helper is exposed for withholding: incorrect Pub 15-T brackets

`src/lib/tax/usFederalPayroll.ts` is a **different** implementation, exported via `workspaceExports.ts`, and no direct posting caller was found in this trace. The capability document labels live payroll calculations internal/demo, so do not infer it is used by `payRunService` or by an actual filing. Its asserted source however is IRS Pub 15-T and some tests call themselves independently hand-calculated while using the same wrong bracket figures.

For a 2026 single employee paid $100,000 annually, W-4 Step 2 unchecked, no credits or other adjustments: IRS Worksheet 1A deducts $8,600 = **$91,400 adjusted wages**. The [IRS 2026 Pub 15-T automated percentage table](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15t) for adjusted wages $57,900–$113,200 prescribes $5,800 + 22% × ($91,400 − $57,900) = **$13,170**. The helper returns **$12,530**, $640 lower. For Step 2 checked and $52,000 annual wages, the IRS table is $2,900 + 22% × ($52,000 − $33,250) = **$7,025**; the helper returns **$6,705**, $320 lower. See source lines around B2026_SINGLE_STD and derived Step-2 table, and probe. Other status and 2025 rows require full row-by-row source review; the IRS MFJ first 2026 threshold is $19,300 while code has $14,900, and HOH first threshold $15,550 while code has $12,700. This API has no W-4 Step 3 or 4 inputs and cannot independently implement all Worksheet 1A cases.

The existing `us-federal-payroll-golden.test.ts` passes while its MFJ, HOH and checked examples encode figures that differ from the official table. A self-consistency or two-job identity test is not a substitute for comparing every published threshold and base amount to the IRS source. Gate the helper from statutory use, transcribe every applicable official row and add independent reviewed boundary and W-4 fixture tests.

## PY-03 — P2: 2026 contractor list uses an obsolete $600 threshold

`payRunPosting.ts` declares `NEC_THRESHOLD_CENTS = 60000` and `contractorsToReport` applies it regardless of requested year. Probe: $800 paid to one contractor during 2026 is included; the ordinary 2026 1099-NEC floor is **$2,000** according to the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/payroll-professionals-tax-center-information-for-payroll-professionals-and-their-clients). $800 is below that particular floor, though eligibility, exceptions and voluntary reporting are separate questions. `PayrollView.tsx` uses this list. Existing tests explicitly expect the $600 2026 behavior. The app has a separate dated threshold module; future-year inflation and legal exceptions need their own verification as previously recorded in AR-05. Treat this as a list-selection false positive, not proof that a return was filed.

## CA-01 — P1 for historical rate usage: unknown HST period silently returns GST only

For a standard taxable $1,000 New Brunswick supply on 1 June 2015, subject to ordinary HST and ignoring special/transitional rules, CRA shows **13% HST = $130** ([CRA rate history](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html)). The pure `canadaSalesTaxRateTable()` output is **5% GST = $50**, $80 lower. `caSalesTax.ts` has a 5% GST row from 2008 and no NB 13% HST row before 1 July 2016; the rate table interprets absence of HST as GST-only, returning a confident result rather than unknown. Newfoundland and Labrador share this missing-era pattern, while other province history also needs checking. The standalone table is exported and tested, but this trace found no confirmed invoice UI caller; impact is conditional on a caller using it for that history. Do not claim 2015 was actually filed. Missing histories should return an explicit unknown/incomplete result, not a partial tax; verify all province/date/category ranges and source effective dates with qualified review.
